## 01 The visit
The technician should go to station S04 first which has had high daytime temperatures since the post it is mounted on was repainted on 15 March, but it gives nighttime readings that are equal to those of other stations. The daytime temperature has grown from +0.1°C above the others to +3.4°C above them, which indicates the growing problem. This issue cannot be fixed in the data, because it depends on how strong the sunlight is. The paint may have covered the vents of the radiation shield, making the shield heat up under the sunlight. Therefore, first, the technician should examine the radiation shield vents for any paint, then clean the vents or install a new shield and measure the temperature using a portable thermometer in the middle of the day. Station S03 can wait, because its bad readings (−40°C, 85°C, and the same value repeated for three days) are easy to spot and remove.

## 02 Least certain call
My least certain call is excluding the temperature measurements at station S04 from 17 March, as those may have been influenced by the painting on 15 March. My reasoning lies in the fact that the temperature issue arose at the same time as the painting work. Yet, in the first week, the daytime temperature difference compared to other stations was just +0.6°C, which is relatively low. In other words, some of the data I am excluding may be accurate. In order to verify my choice, I would look for any photos or records showing when paint got on the radiation shield. This would help determine whether 17 March was the right date to start excluding the temperature data.

## 03 Where the AI was wrong
At first, Claude said the painting at the S04 station had “no effect worth flagging”, since it was based on the average temperature for both day and night. However, I realized that the afternoon temperatures on 10–11 April were +4°C to +7°C higher at the S04 station. After checking the readings in the daytime and at night separately, I noticed that the temperature error was increasing over time in the daytime only.
The initial check.py made by Claude flagged the S01 pressure as faulty 12 times, while in the same output it also marked S01 pressure as “keep”. This contradiction made me look closer: S01 is situated 1,300 metres higher than the other stations, so its pressure naturally behaves differently, and those 12 flags were false alarms.

## 04 Next year
The improvements I would make to the system include displaying a single simple summary for each station with its status as either OK, needs a correction in the data, or requires a visit. The system needs to compare the readings at each station with those of its nearest stations to identify any discrepancies. It needs to alert only in case there is a new issue or in case an already existing problem becomes more serious. I would eliminate minor alerts and also stop trying to read the free-text maintenance notes automatically. But I would not allow the system to automatically choose the station that requires the visit of the technician, or to apply corrections to the data without a person checking them first.

## 05 By hand
For S04 (Foothills Community Garden) located at coordinates 39.754°N latitude and −105.146° longitude, the time for solar noon on 20 March 2025 was calculated from the formula: Solar Noon (UTC) = 12:00 - (Longitude ÷ 15) - Equation of Time. For the longitude the calculation would be: −105.146 × 4 = −420.584 minutes, or 7 hours and 35 seconds. The equation of time on 20 March 2025 is approximately −7.5 minutes, thus the solar noon would be 12:00 + 7:00:35 + 0:07:30 = 19:08 UTC. It is consistent with the output from the NOAA Solar Calculator. In the S04 sunlight data, the maximum value of sunlight is recorded at 19:40 UTC, which is 32 minutes after the calculated solar noon. This is fairly close and could be explained with cloudiness or variations in the atmosphere. This comparison can be used for checking the data logger clock since the maximum sunlight measurement should happen around the solar noon. For S06, the sunlight peak is 6 hours off indicating that their logger clock was misadjusted after changing batteries.
